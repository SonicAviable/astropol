The game has left early access. Thank you to everyone who played, left feedback and reported bugs — this release would not exist without you.
# CHRONICLES AND VOICES
The galaxy has found its voice.
- Faction chronicle. Before every game a narrator tells the story of your people: how they arose, what they went through and why they reached for the stars. Illustrations and subtitles on screen, styled as an old manuscript.
- Every faction has its own theme. Six orchestral themes, one per people.
- Rulers speak. 228 diplomacy lines are voiced, every ruler with a voice of their own. The Xarn Dominion's marshal sounds exactly as rough as you imagined.
- Voiced tutorial. The advisor explains the first steps out loud, not just in text.
# LIVING COSMOS
Something now lives in the void between the stars.
- Space fauna: rays, jellyfish, the Spark Swarm, crystalids and the mysterious Wanderer. Each species has its own habits, its own voices and a tooltip on hover.
- Fauna stays hidden until you discover it: explore the galaxy to meet its inhabitants.
- New ships: Xarn and Iridian Oracle science vessels (the Oracle's has a spinning gyroscope), the Astrea AS/07 science ship and the Xarn destroyer "Valiant".
- Black holes are now ray-traced: light bends around the shadow, with thin disk jets and a bright photon ring.
- A new galactic core: a barred bulge, arms that follow the galaxy's shape, dust lanes.
# GAMEPLAY
- Supply. Titanium and alloys now reach the treasury along hyperlanes. Cut the enemy's routes and their economy will choke.
- Era map and research plan. Technologies are laid out by era. Pick any goal, even a locked one, and the game will chart a path and research everything in order.
- 35 new technology icons: weapons, defense, industry, construction, hyperdrives, logistics and doctrines.
- Political map. Just empire borders, their large names and emblems, nothing else.
- Colony and starbase markers above stars are visible at any zoom level.
- Clicking a planet opens its overview right away, with no window in between.
# THE ATLAS INTERFACE
We redrew the whole interface in a single style: brass, dark enamel, serif type.
- Astrolabe buttons: a brass frame with gems that tell what the action does. Sapphire — main action, emerald — create, ruby — danger.
- Observatory windows: every large window has its own engraving in the background.
- Diplomacy "Holographic Hall": ruler portraits, relations as cards, agreements as tiles.
- New look for the main and pause menus, the top resource bar with brass medallions and the minimap, research windows and the planner.
- The colony screen with districts fanned out like cards; the build menu and the ship designer with its arsenal; the "Dry Dock" orbital shipyard.
- Events and anomalies, the game summary and the music player are restyled too.
- Icons are sharper and text is no longer blurry.
# PERFORMANCE
The bigger your empire, the smoother the game now runs.
- Up to 2.4× more FPS in the late game. In our test on Ultra after 12 in-game years it went from 44 FPS to 105.
- Every CPU core. Multithreaded rendering. The Spark Swarm and asteroids are computed in parallel. The AI estimates fleet strength instantly instead of by brute force.
- Stars and labels off screen are no longer updated for nothing.
- Fast loading: no delay, shaders are warmed up in advance, no hitches at the start of a game.
- Your GPU gets a rest: 30 FPS while you are idle and 15 FPS when the window is minimized.
# FIXES
- AI empires no longer lose their bonuses and personality when loading a save made in another game language.
- The AI goes bankrupt by the same rules as the player.
- Constructors, scouts and science ships no longer get stuck on broken routes.
- Pulling a constructor away cancels the construction and refunds the alloys, with no double payment.
- The Spark Swarm no longer teleports, and "Attack" hits the nearest fleet.
- Ruler lines are no longer cut off mid-word.
- Text in diplomacy and the research window no longer overflows its frame.
- Dozens of small layout and text fixes.
